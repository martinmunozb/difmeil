How to Manage Multiple Gmail Accounts in One Inbox

Personal email, client work, a side project. A shared inbox view can help you see what needs attention without checking each account in turn. The right setup depends on where you read your mail and how you want to handle it.

The quick answer

On a phone, use All inboxes in the Gmail app. On a computer, Gmail lets you switch between accounts, but that does not combine their inboxes. To bring incoming copies into one Gmail account, use forwarding. For a combined view that works with the original Gmail accounts in Chrome, consider an extension such as Difmeil.

First, decide what “one inbox” should mean

There are two different goals here. You might want a shared view: messages stay in their own accounts, but you read and manage them through one interface. Or you might want mail delivered to a main account: new messages from other addresses arrive there as forwarded copies.

Imagine you have personal, freelance and project Gmail accounts. Seeing all three together helps you triage the day. Keeping their identities clear matters just as much: archiving a project message should affect the right mailbox, and a client reply should come from your work address.

  • Mostly on your phone? Start with Gmail’s own combined mobile view.
  • Only checking a second account occasionally? Account switching may be enough.
  • Want new mail delivered to a primary Gmail address? Consider forwarding.
  • Need to manage the original accounts together on a computer? Consider a multi-account extension or email client.

A unified view does not merge Google identities, Drive files, account settings or storage quotas. You still own and sign in to each account separately.

Option 1: switch between Gmail accounts on desktop

Google supports signing in to several accounts in the same browser session. Open Gmail, select your profile picture or initial, add another account and sign in. Use that menu to choose the mailbox you want. You do not need to sign out each time.

This is a sensible choice when you prefer separate inboxes. You can keep mailboxes in separate tabs, or use Chrome profiles when you want a broader separation of browser activity. The tradeoff is that checking every inbox remains a separate task.

“Multiple Inboxes” is a different feature

Gmail’s desktop Multiple Inboxes layout builds sections using searches or labels within the mailbox you are viewing. It is useful for organizing that mailbox; it does not, by itself, connect separate Google accounts. Likewise, signing in to several accounts does not create the mobile app’s All inboxes view on the web.

Google’s documentation: sign in to multiple accounts and Gmail inbox layouts.

Option 2: use All inboxes in the Gmail mobile app

Gmail for Android, iPhone and iPad has a built-in combined view. Add your Gmail accounts through your profile menu using Add another account, then choose Google and sign in. Once added, open the menu at the top left and select All inboxes.

This is often the easiest starting point if you handle email on your phone. It shows inbox mail together without forwarding it to a main address. Adding accounts in the app does not create a combined inbox at mail.google.com on your computer.

Official instructions: Gmail for Android and Gmail for iPhone and iPad.

Option 3: forward new messages to a main Gmail account

Forwarding suits people who want one destination for new mail and are comfortable managing copies. In each source Gmail account, open Settings → See all settings → Forwarding and POP/IMAP (or Forwarding). Add the destination address, complete verification there, then return to the source settings to enable forwarding and save. Choose what happens to the source copy.

Automatic forwarding covers new mail except spam, rather than moving your existing history. Filters can forward selected messages. Reading or archiving a forwarded copy does not synchronize that action back to the original mailbox.

Check the address you reply from

Forwarding does not configure the sending identity. If you want replies to use another Gmail address you own, set up and verify Gmail’s Send mail as separately, then check the From address before sending.

See Google’s forwarding instructions and Send mail as guidance. For a work or school account, check your organization’s forwarding policy.

Following an older tutorial?

Google is retiring Gmailify and POP-based “Check mail from other accounts” for third-party accounts. Its published timeline says new users are no longer supported after the first quarter of 2026; existing users can continue until January 2027. These changes are distinct from Gmail-to-Gmail forwarding and the mobile All inboxes view. Check Google’s current transition guidance before relying on an old POP setup.

Option 4: use a browser extension for a unified Gmail view

A multi-account extension can give you a shared workspace while keeping messages associated with their original accounts. Before installing one, check which providers it supports, which mail actions it offers, how authorization works and which features require payment. An unread counter alone is different from a full inbox you can work in.

How to set up Difmeil with multiple Gmail accounts

Use these steps in Chrome on a computer. The control names below are from Difmeil’s English interface; Google’s sign-in screens can vary with your account and security settings.

  1. Install from the official listing

    Open Difmeil in the Chrome Web Store, choose Add to Chrome and review Chrome’s installation prompt. Confirm if you want to proceed. Open Difmeil from Chrome’s Extensions menu; you can pin its icon for easier access.

  2. Add your first Gmail account

    Click the Difmeil icon. In the initial Connect your first account view, select Add an account. This starts Google OAuth: choose the Google account you want to connect and complete any sign-in or two-step verification Google requests.

  3. Review Google’s access request

    Review the consent screen before approving. The current implementation requests basic identity information, permission to read and manage Gmail messages, permission to send mail, and read-only access to Google’s “Other contacts” for sender photos and recipient suggestions. These support the inbox, mail actions and composing. Difmeil does not ask you to type your Google password into the extension; sign-in happens with Google.

    Read the privacy policy if you want to understand data handling first. Mail requests go between your browser and Google. An authorization service exchanges and refreshes tokens without storing them. If a managed account blocks access, ask your administrator about the policy.

  4. Connect the other accounts

    After the first account connects, open the popup’s account controls and choose Add an account again. Authorize each additional Google account separately. Check the account you select each time; being signed in to Gmail in Chrome does not automatically grant Difmeil access. The full-page sidebar also has an Add account control.

  5. Open the combined inbox

    Choose Open full view in the popup, then All accounts in the full-page sidebar to work across your connected accounts. Select an individual account when you want to focus on it. Start with a few recent messages and check the account identity before applying actions.

  6. Read, act and adjust notifications

    Open a message to read it. Use the read/unread, archive or trash actions as needed; check the From account when composing or replying. Open Settings to review Notifications and the per-account notification switches in My accounts. Basic notifications are available on the free plan; advanced rules and do-not-disturb schedules require Pro.

You can continue opening Gmail directly when you need it. Installing Difmeil does not require setting up automatic forwarding or replacing your existing Gmail addresses.

A simple routine for working across accounts

A shared inbox is most useful when it gives you a clear next action. Try this routine before adding more rules or changing your whole email setup.

  • Scan together, focus separately. Use All accounts to spot urgent messages, then select your work account for a focused session.
  • Check identity before sending. A personal conversation and a client request can sit next to each other. Look at the account and recipient before replying.
  • Archive after deciding. Keep the inbox for messages that still need attention, rather than using unread status as your only task list.
  • Be selective about alerts. Enable notifications for accounts where interruptions are useful. If Gmail or another mail app is also notifying you, review both to avoid duplicate alerts.
  • Test your setup. Send yourself a harmless test message, confirm which account receives it, and verify that your chosen action appears in that account in Gmail.

If an account or message is missing

The combined view only shows one account

Check that you connected each account through Google, rather than only signing in to Gmail in a browser tab. In Difmeil, select All accounts and check whether an account is hidden from the inbox. Clear any account or unread filters that could narrow the list.

An account asks to reconnect

Complete the offered reconnection flow with the correct Google account. If authorization fails, read Google’s error message; an organization policy or a withdrawn permission may require a different next step. Do not repeatedly change passwords just because a mailbox needs authorization again.

You cannot find an older message

The popup searches loaded messages. Try the full-page Gmail search for a broader lookup, or open the original account in Gmail to confirm that the message exists there. Forwarding, by contrast, does not bring your entire past mailbox into the destination automatically.

You are not receiving notifications

Check Difmeil’s global and per-account notification settings, then Chrome and your operating system’s notification settings. Quiet modes can suppress alerts. Notifications help you notice incoming mail; they should not be your only way to check an important mailbox. For product issues, use Difmeil support.

Frequently asked questions

Can I combine two Gmail accounts without merging them?

Yes. Gmail mobile’s All inboxes and a multi-account interface such as Difmeil let you view accounts together while keeping their identities separate. This does not merge the Google accounts or their storage.

Where is All inboxes in Gmail on my computer?

Gmail’s web interface offers account switching and inbox layouts, including Multiple Inboxes. It does not offer the same cross-account All inboxes view described in Google’s mobile instructions. Use the Gmail app on a phone, forwarding for incoming copies, or a desktop multi-account interface.

Do I need to forward my email to use Difmeil?

No. You authorize each Gmail account with Google OAuth. Difmeil works with those connected accounts, so a forwarding rule is not part of its setup.

Can I reply from the right Gmail address?

Difmeil supports replies and composing from connected accounts. Check the sending account before sending. If you instead collect forwarded messages in one Gmail mailbox, configure Gmail’s Send mail as separately for another address you own.

Is Difmeil free for multiple accounts?

The free plan supports up to three Gmail accounts, a unified inbox, full-page reading, search and basic notifications. More accounts and advanced notification and personalization features require Pro. See the plan comparison.

Does Difmeil support Outlook or work on my phone?

Difmeil is a Chrome extension for Gmail accounts. It is not a mobile app and does not connect Outlook, Yahoo or general IMAP mailboxes. Gmail’s mobile app or a client with explicit provider support may suit those needs.

Can I use a work or school Gmail account?

A Google Workspace Gmail account may be subject to your organization’s third-party access rules. Check with your administrator if Google blocks authorization. Do not assume that owning the address means you can override those policies.
