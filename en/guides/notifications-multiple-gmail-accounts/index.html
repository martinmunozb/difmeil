How to Get Notifications from Multiple Gmail Accounts

You can be signed in to several Gmail accounts and still miss an important message. The solution is to check how each account sends alerts, what your device allows, and which notifications actually deserve your attention.

The quick answer

You do not have to keep switching accounts to receive alerts. On desktop, configure Gmail’s desktop notifications for each mailbox and allow notifications in the browser and operating system. On mobile, review notification settings for every account in the Gmail app. In Chrome, Difmeil offers another approach: connect the accounts once and manage their notifications alongside a unified inbox.

Start with Gmail’s own desktop notifications

Account switching and notification delivery are separate things. You can stay signed in to several Google accounts; viewing one mailbox does not mean the others have been signed out. What matters is whether each mailbox has alerts configured and whether the browser can show them.

  1. Open the Gmail account you want to configure.
  2. Go to Settings → See all settings, then find Desktop notifications.
  3. Choose new-mail or important-mail alerts and save your changes.
  4. Allow the site to notify you if the browser requests permission. Repeat the Gmail setting for the other accounts.

Google describes these alerts for Gmail opened and signed in within a supported browser. Do not assume they will arrive with Gmail closed. With inbox categories enabled, new-mail alerts cover Primary; important-mail alerts use Gmail’s importance classification. See Google’s desktop notification instructions.

For an occasional second mailbox, this may be all you need. Before installing anything, test your current setup with a new message to each account and see whether it meets your needs.

Check the browser and operating system too

Gmail’s notification preference is one layer. Chrome’s site permission and your operating system’s notification settings are others. A mail setting cannot override a blocked browser permission or a device’s quiet mode.

For Gmail website alerts in Chrome, open Settings → Privacy and security → Site settings → Notifications and review the permission for Gmail. You can also inspect the permission through the site-information control beside the address bar. Check that your operating system allows Chrome notifications, then review Focus or Do Not Disturb settings.

Chrome may remove permissions from unused sites, so recheck the site permission if alerts used to work. This website permission is different from an extension’s notification capability: allowing Gmail’s site does not configure Difmeil’s per-account switches. Read Chrome’s notification guidance for the current controls.

A useful test is to keep Gmail open, turn off device quiet mode temporarily and send one harmless message to each mailbox. If only one account stays silent, investigate its settings first; if every account stays silent, inspect the shared browser or device layer.

On mobile, configure every Gmail account

The Gmail app can notify you about more than one account. Review the settings for each account you added; the account currently visible on screen is not the only one that can receive alerts.

Android

In Gmail, open the menu, go to Settings, select an account and review Email notifications. For label-specific alerts, review that label’s synchronization and notification settings. If nothing arrives, check Gmail sync and Android’s app notification permissions. Google’s Android instructions explain the available levels.

iPhone and iPad

Allow Gmail notifications in the device settings, then review Email notifications inside Gmail’s settings for each account and choose a level that suits you. See Google’s iPhone and iPad instructions.

All inboxes is a reading view, not a master notification switch. Bringing messages together in that view does not replace per-account alert preferences. Mobile notifications and desktop notifications also need separate setup; changing one should not be treated as a way to configure the other.

Which notification setup fits your workflow?

Choose where you want to be interrupted before choosing a tool. A phone alert may be enough for occasional mail; a desktop workspace can be more useful when you need to act on messages immediately.

Set up notifications across your accounts in Difmeil

Difmeil is a Chrome extension for Gmail accounts. It checks connected mailboxes in the background and can show native system notifications without a Gmail tab open. It uses periodic checks, so alerts are not guaranteed to be instant. Chrome needs to be running, the extension enabled, and the account authorized.

  1. Install the official extension

    Open Difmeil in the Chrome Web Store, choose Add to Chrome and review the installation prompt. Open Difmeil from Chrome’s Extensions menu; pin it if you want quick access.

  2. Connect each account with Google

    Select Add an account in the popup and complete Google OAuth for the Gmail account you want to use. Repeat through the account controls for your other accounts. Sign-in happens with Google; do not enter your password into the extension. For the full walkthrough, see connecting multiple Gmail accounts.

    Review the consent request: the implementation asks for basic identity information, Gmail read/manage and send permissions, and read-only access to Other contacts for sender photos and recipient suggestions. Difmeil is an email client as well as a notifier, so access is broader than a simple counter. Read the privacy policy before authorizing. Mail requests go between the browser and Google; an authorization service exchanges and refreshes tokens without storing them.

  3. Enable the alerts you want

    Open Settings → Notifications and review the main notification switch. In My accounts, check the notification switch for each connected account. An account hidden from the inbox is excluded from notifications too, so keep the accounts you want monitored visible.

  4. Check device settings and test

    Allow Chrome notifications in your operating system and review its quiet modes. Leave Chrome running and send a new test message to each account. Use a fresh test message: existing unread messages may already have been notified or excluded by settings; unread counts do not guarantee a new alert.

  5. Choose how to act on mail

    The toolbar popup gives a quick view; Open full view opens the full-page inbox. Select All accounts to see messages together or select one account to focus. Read, reply, mark messages read, archive or move them to trash as needed.

Avoid running two noisy alert systems by accident. Once you have tested the setup, decide whether you want Gmail website alerts, Difmeil alerts or both. Turning one off is a workflow choice, not a requirement for the other to function.

What is free, and what requires Pro?

The free plan supports up to three Gmail accounts, a unified inbox, full-page reading, search and basic notifications. You can enable or disable notifications globally and for individual accounts.

Pro supports more accounts and adds advanced notification controls: do-not-disturb schedules, sender and subject rules, category filters and additional per-account overrides. If your priority is simply noticing mail from two or three accounts, start with the basic controls. If you need client messages to interrupt you while routine updates stay quiet, review the advanced rules.

Pro preferences are retained when access expires, but premium rules stop applying; the free controls remain available. Review your alert setup after a plan change rather than assuming an old premium filter is still active. The current plan comparison is the reference for included features.

Start with a small number of useful alerts. For example, keep one work account enabled during a focused session and silence a low-priority project account. More notifications are not always a better setup.

Troubleshoot one layer at a time

Only one account is silent

Confirm that it is connected and authorized. Check its own notification switch, whether it is hidden, and any applicable rules. If Gmail itself is silent, inspect that account’s Gmail notification level and mail categories. Use a fresh test message instead of expecting an old unread message to trigger again.

All accounts are silent

Check the global notification switch, operating system permissions and quiet mode. In Difmeil, confirm that Chrome is running, the extension is enabled and you have a network connection. Periodic checking can be delayed by browser or device conditions; do not rely on email alerts as a guaranteed real-time channel.

You receive duplicate alerts

Identify which apps sent them. Gmail, Difmeil and a phone can each alert you about the same message. Disable the redundant source if you do not need it, rather than disconnecting a working account.

A rule seems to ignore an important message

Review the rule against the message’s sender, subject and category. In Difmeil, blocked senders take precedence over inclusion rules, and a read message is not notified. When troubleshooting, simplify the rules first, then reintroduce them one at a time. If you need help, contact Difmeil support without sending private message bodies.

Frequently asked questions

Can Gmail notify me about two accounts at the same time?

Yes. Configure each account’s notification preferences. On mobile, review each added account in Gmail and allow app notifications. On desktop, check Gmail’s mailbox settings, browser permission and device settings.

Do I have to switch to an account to get its notifications?

No. Switching the mailbox you are viewing is different from configuring its alerts. Keep the relevant accounts signed in or connected, and check the conditions of the app you use.

Does Difmeil need Gmail tabs open?

No. Its background checks do not require an open Gmail tab. Chrome must be running, Difmeil enabled and the account connected. Alerts depend on periodic checks and device notification settings.

Will Difmeil notify me if Chrome is completely closed?

Do not expect that. Difmeil runs as a Chrome extension, not as a separate desktop notification service. Keep Chrome running when you want it to check your mail.

Can I mute just one Gmail account?

Yes. Difmeil’s basic per-account notification switch is available on the free plan. Advanced per-account overrides and notification schedules require Pro. Gmail mobile also has account-specific notification preferences.

Why do I see unread messages but receive no alert?

An unread count is not a promise of a new notification. A message may already have been notified, read before a check or excluded by settings. Use a fresh message to test the setup.

Does All inboxes turn on notifications for every mobile account?

No. It combines the reading view. Check notification settings for every account and the Gmail app’s device permission separately.
